# The S.E.T. Framework — Social Engineering Taxonomy

Social engineering works because it targets human instinct, not software. The S.E.T. framework categorizes the psychological triggers attackers use:

| Tactic | How it works | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Urgency / Pressure** | Manufactures time pressure to suppress critical thinking | "Your account will be deleted in 24 hours!" |
| **Intimidation** | Uses threats or fear of consequences | "Legal action will be taken against you." |
| **Pretexting** | Invents a believable role or story to obtain information | "Hi, I'm from IT and I need your password to fix your account." |
| **Consensus / Crowd validation** | "Everyone else is doing it" social proof | "All your coworkers have already completed this form." |
| **Authority** | Impersonates perceived power (IT, police, boss, bank) | Emails signed "— Director of Finance" |
| **Familiarity** | Exploits trust of known names, brands, or relationships | Looks like it's from a coworker's real address |
| **Scarcity** | Limited availability pushes impulsive action | "Only 2 spots left — claim yours now." |

**Defense:** verify through a *known* channel before acting. Never use contact info supplied by the message itself.
